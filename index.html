01 — Issue

The faster systems evolve,
the more their trust boundaries matter.

AI has made software faster to build and easier to connect.
Every new connection brings a new assumption worth testing.

01

The real attack path stays out of view

Individual checks pass while the chain between identity, data, and authority remains untested.

02

Permissions drift across connected services

A valid session reaches a capability that the original trust boundary should never have granted.

03

Agents inherit more authority than they need

Untrusted input can influence how a model uses tools, retrieves data, or takes actions.

04

Scanner output gets mistaken for impact

A severity label arrives before the finding has been reproduced and challenged with controls.

05

The report ends before the fix is testable

Engineering teams need a clear root cause and a way to verify that the boundary holds.

06

More automation creates more uncertainty

Fast testing is useful when scope, evidence, and human judgment remain part of the process.

07 — Process

Ask precisely. Test carefully.
Keep the evidence.

Every engagement follows a clear research process.

  1. Step 01

    Understand

    Learn the product, the assets you control, and the security question that matters.

  2. Step 02

    Scope

    Agree on permissions, accounts, constraints, testing limits, and deliverables.

  3. Step 03

    Investigate

    Follow the behavior with manual research and carefully bounded automation.

  4. Step 04

    Validate

    Use negative controls, repeatable evidence, and realistic impact analysis.

  5. Step 05

    Deliver

    Explain the root cause and define remediation and regression checks.

  • Prove the impact with the minimum necessary evidence
  • Use automation to support careful investigation
  • Keep human review at every submission decision
  • Share reasoning, limitations, and a path to remediation

10 — Philosophy

Look beyond the surface.
Follow the system.
Let the evidence decide.

AI can produce thousands of hypotheses.
Research asks which one the evidence supports.

A useful investigation follows a weakness beyond its first symptom, then challenges its own conclusions.

We distinguish a live observation from a simulation, a submitted claim from a confirmed finding, and an apparent impact from one we can defend.

That discipline is the foundation of this practice.

12 — FAQ

A few questions
before we start.

Get in touch even if your scope is still taking shape.

Can we talk before the scope is fully defined?+

Yes. Start with your product, the assets you control, and the questions you want answered. We can then agree on written scope, testing permissions, deliverables, and commercial terms.

Do you work with AI labs and product teams?+

The practice welcomes scoped evaluation and research opportunities with AI labs and product teams, including products built on Claude, GPT, and other models. No affiliation with Anthropic or OpenAI is claimed.

Can we request just an application or API assessment?+

Yes. Services can be scoped independently. The assessment can focus on a specific application, identity flow, API, authorization boundary, or business-logic concern.

How is sensitive research handled?+

Testing follows written authorization and program scope. Detailed evidence is shared through the agreed channel, and publication follows coordinated disclosure. These website summaries omit operational secrets and runnable exploit instructions.

What do the research statuses mean?+

Resolved, accepted, submitted, and in review represent different stages. A submitted or in-review report is not presented as vendor-confirmed. Statuses reflect the researcher’s documented report history; the individual case summaries explain their sources and limitations.

How are pricing and timelines decided?+

They depend on scope, complexity, access, and the depth of testing. Share your goals and preferred timeline so that scope and commercial terms can be discussed before work begins.

What should we prepare for the first conversation?+

A short brief covering your product, assets you control, desired outcomes, and preferred timeline. Avoid sending credentials or sensitive evidence in the first message.
